# VESPER music review — proposal 01: Canticle

**Approved and integrated into VESPER.** The production synth, desktop player,
firmware and full demo video now use Canticle. The files here preserve the
original review comparison; `production_hashes.json` and `review_metrics.json`
describe the pre-integration review. See `integration.json` for the release and
the exact audio-match verification.

## What went wrong in the original

Review of the actual sequenced notes confirms unintended clashes. Times below
refer to the original demo, not the comparison file.

| Time | Pad harmony | Lead note | Conflict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:08.672 | B-flat major: B-flat, D, F | D-flat 5 | Minor third against the pad's major third D |
| 0:16.172 | F major: F, A, C | A-flat 5 | Minor third against A |
| 0:23.672 | C major: C, E, G | E-flat 5 | Minor third against E |

These repeat with the 30-second harmonic cycle. Other notes also fall outside
the stated D-natural-minor key. Out-of-key notes can be musically useful, but
these were generated by an incorrect transposition rule rather than composed
as prepared and resolved tensions.

The rule increments both offsets 14 and 15 when the chord is major. Raising
offset 15 to 16 changes a minor third to a major third, as intended. Raising
14 to 15 changes a second to a minor third, introducing the very clash the
rule was meant to remove. Blindly transposing the remaining phrase also puts
A-flat over B-flat and E-flat over F.

There is another structural mistake: the lead triggers on even sixteenth-note
steps, and the pattern rotation is also even. Consequently only the eight even
entries of the sixteen-entry melody table ever play. The resulting repeated
eighth-note pattern contains large jumps, including a nine-semitone drop from
C6 to E-flat5 over F, with almost no space for an answer or a cadence.

The bright FM sound and high register make those note choices prominent. The
original delay also combines an eighth-note tap with a two-thirds-of-a-beat tap,
adding rhythmic density. Its FM ratio is harmonic (2:1), and the rendered signal
does not hit the digital full-scale limit; wrong notes and phrasing are the main
identified problems, rather than evidence of a clipping fault.

## The proposed direction

Canticle keeps the D minor / B-flat / F / C progression, 128 BPM, the bass and
drum patterns, and all 64 bars and arrangement boundaries. It proposes:

- **Explicit melody notes:** four related four-bar phrases instead of automatic
  chord-root transposition. All melody notes belong to D natural minor.
- **A narrower, lower register:** E4–F5, with short calls, answers, rests and
  phrase endings. Passing notes remain, so this is not restricted to arpeggios.
- **Closer pad voicings:** common upper notes survive harmonic changes rather
  than moving the entire chord shape up or down.
- **A softer bell:** less phase modulation, a short attack ramp and a longer
  decay, with lower trigger levels.
- **Less persistent echoes:** eighth-note and approximately sixteenth-note
  taps, with lower cross-feedback.

This changes melody, voicing and timbre together; it is a proposed musical
revision, not an A/B test isolating only the erroneous pitches. It deliberately
leaves the existing rhythmic arrangement available for comparison. Listening
approval was received before adoption.

## Files and verification

`synth_original.c` is an exact snapshot of the production source.
`synth_proposal.c` is the isolated candidate; `make_proposal.py` regenerates it.
`render_audio.c` compiles either synth into a standalone WAV renderer.

Both complete scores were rendered with signed-overflow trapping enabled.
The original WAV matches the production player's WAV byte for byte. The
proposal has no full-scale samples, and all explicit melody notes pass the
key/range check. `review_metrics.json` includes the original note events,
audio measurements, and comparison gains. `production_hashes.json` records the
source, player, video and UF2 as they were during review. Before approval,
packaging required those files to remain unchanged; after integration it reports
which still match that historical snapshot.

From the repository root, to regenerate:

```powershell
python 18_Vesper/music_review/make_proposal.py
gcc -O2 -ftrapv -std=c11 -I18_Vesper/vesper 18_Vesper/music_review/render_audio.c 18_Vesper/music_review/synth_original.c -o 18_Vesper/music_review/render_original.exe -lm
gcc -O2 -ftrapv -std=c11 -I18_Vesper/vesper 18_Vesper/music_review/render_audio.c 18_Vesper/music_review/synth_proposal.c -o 18_Vesper/music_review/render_proposal.exe -lm
18_Vesper/music_review/render_original.exe 18_Vesper/music_review/original.wav
18_Vesper/music_review/render_proposal.exe 18_Vesper/music_review/canticle_proposal.wav
python 18_Vesper/music_review/package_audio.py
```
